1. THE LACRIMAL SYSTEM (EPIPHORA & DACRYOCYSTITIS)★★★★

1.1 Anatomy & Evaluation of Epiphora

  • Drainage Pathway: Puncta $\rightarrow$ Canaliculi $\rightarrow$ Lacrimal Sac $\rightarrow$ Nasolacrimal Duct (NLD) $\rightarrow$ opens into Inferior Nasal Meatus (guarded by Valve of Hasner).
  • Lacrimal Pump: Driven by Orbicularis oculi (CN VII); paralysis $\rightarrow$ pump failure epiphora.
  • Causes of Epiphora (Obstructive vs. Pump):
    • Punctal: Stenosis or Lower Lid Ectropion.
    • Canalicular: Obstruction / Actinomyces canaliculitis.
    • Nasolacrimal Duct (NLD — #1): Congenital membrane or Adult acquired stenosis (Chronic Dacryocystitis).
    • Pump Failure: Facial nerve (CN VII) palsy.
  • Evaluation of Epiphora (4 Steps): (1) Slit-lamp inspection (rule out trichiasis/ectropion), (2) Lacrirnal Sac Regurgitation Test (+ve reflux of mucopus on pressing sac = Chronic Dacryocystitis; 🚫 "Negative regurgitation test" is FALSE in chronic dacryocystitis!), (3) Fluorescein Dye Disappearance Test (best in infants), (4) Diagnostic Syringing & Probing.

1.2 Congenital vs. Adult Chronic Dacryocystitis

  • Congenital Dacryocystitis (Neonatal Epiphora):
    • Cause: Imperforate membrane at Valve of Hasner.
    • DD of Neonatal Watering (3): (1) Congenital NLD obstruction (#1) (+ve mucopus regurgitation, clear normal cornea), (2) Congenital Glaucoma / Buphthalmos (photophobia + large hazy cornea >13 mm), (3) Ophthalmia neonatorum / Corneal abrasion.
    • Rx by Age: Up to 6–9 months (First-line!) = Lacrimal Sac Massage + Topical Antibiotics | Persistent >6–9 months = Probing & Syringing.
  • Adult Chronic Dacryocystitis:
    • Organism (#1): Streptococcus pneumoniae (Pneumococcus) (or Staph. aureus).
    • Complications: Causes persistent unilateral conjunctivitis, Pneumococcal Hypopyon Corneal Ulcer, and post-op Endophthalmitis! (🚫 Cataract surgery is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED until treated!).
    • Rx of Choice: Dacryocystorhinostomy (DCR) $\rightarrow$ bony opening made into the Middle Nasal Meatus! (Or Dacryocystectomy [DCT] in frail elderly).
2. THE ORBIT (PROPTOSIS, GRAVES' & INFECTIONS)★★★★★

2.1 Orbital Anatomy, Blow-Out Fx & Proptosis

  • Orbital Septum: Anterior boundary separating Preseptal Cellulitis (lid swelling from insect bite/trauma; no proptosis, normal motility, Rx: systemic antibiotics) from Orbital Cellulitis.
  • Medial Wall (Thinnest Wall!): Formed by Maxillary, Lacrimal, Ethmoid (lamina papyracea), & Sphenoid. (🚫 Zygoma does NOT form the medial wall!).
  • Superior Orbital Fissure: Transmits CN III, IV, VI, V1 (nasociliary, frontal, lacrimal), ophthalmic veins. (🚫 Facial nerve [CN VII] does NOT pass through SOF!).
  • Blow-Out Fracture: Blunt trauma (tennis ball/fist) $\rightarrow$ fractures Inferior Wall (Orbital Floor) $\rightarrow$ entraps IR/IO muscles $\rightarrow$ Binocular Diplopia (disappears on covering either eye!) + Enophthalmos + Infraorbital anesthesia. (🚫 Does NOT require immediate Day-1 emergency surgery!).
  • Proptosis Rules:
    • #1 Cause Worldwide (Adult Unilateral AND Bilateral): Thyroid Eye Disease (Graves' / Dysthyroid Ophthalmopathy)!
    • Axial Proptosis: Graves', Optic nerve glioma, Orbital cellulitis. (🚫 Lacrimal gland tumor causes NON-AXIAL / Eccentric proptosis!).
    • Painful / Acute Proptosis Causes (4): Orbital Cellulitis, Cavernous Sinus Thrombosis, Orbital Pseudotumor (idiopathic orbital inflammation), Trauma/Hemorrhage, Rhabdomyosarcoma (children).
    • Enophthalmos Causes: Blow-out trauma, Horner's Syndrome (🚫 Horner's NEVER causes proptosis!), Scirrhous Breast Ca metastasis, Cachexia, Post-radiotherapy.

2.2 Thyroid Eye Disease (Graves' / Thyrotoxicosis) ★★★★★

🔬 Autoimmune GAG deposition & edema enlarging extraocular muscle bellies (sparing tendons). Confirmed by Orbital Ultrasonography / CT.
  • Eyelid Signs:
    • Upper Lid Retraction (Dalrymple's Sign) & Scleral Show: #1 Most common sign of Graves' disease! (🚫 Ptosis is NOT a sign of Graves'!).
    • Lid Lag on Downgaze (Von Graefe's Sign).
    • Stellwag's Sign: Infrequent blinking | Enroth's: Lid edema.
  • Extraocular Muscle Involvement Order (I M S L):
    • 1st: Inferior Rectus (restricts elevation $\rightarrow$ diplopia) $\rightarrow$ 2nd: Medial Rectus (causes convergent squint!).
    • Möbius Sign: Convergence weakness.
  • Complications: Axial Exophthalmos $\rightarrow$ Lagophthalmos & Exposure Keratopathy, and Compressive Optic Neuropathy.
  • DD of Thyroid Ophthalmopathy: Orbital cellulitis, Orbital pseudotumor, Cavernous sinus thrombosis, Carotid-cavernous fistula, Orbital tumors (Lymphoma, Glioma, Meningioma).

2.3 Master Differentiation: Orbital Cellulitis vs. Cavernous Sinus Thrombosis ★★★★★

Feature Acute Orbital Cellulitis Cavernous Sinus Thrombosis (CST)
Origin & Site Infection behind orbital septum (🚫 NOT in front!), secondary to Ethmoiditis (#1). Septic thrombosis of cavernous sinus from facial danger triangle, sinusitis, or orbital cellulitis.
Laterality Strictly Unilateral. Starts unilateral $\rightarrow$ rapidly becomes BILATERAL!
Symptoms Severe ocular pain (worse on eye movement), high fever. Profound systemic illness: High fever, severe headache, vomiting, drowsiness / coma.
Clinical Signs Lid edema, chemosis, axial proptosis, limited motility. (Differentiated from Panophthalmitis by Vision: vision is preserved early in cellulitis vs. lost in panophthalmitis). Bilateral proptosis, Mastoid Edema (behind ear!), rapid Cranial Nerve Palsies (CN III, IV, VI — VI earliest!), corneal anesthesia (V1), and Bilateral Papilledema.
Treatment IV Antibiotics + Surgical Drainage Indications (2): (1) Orbital/Subperiosteal Abscess, (2) Deteriorating Vision / no response in 48h. Massive High-dose IV Antibiotics + Systemic Anticoagulation (Heparin) + Corticosteroids.
3. THE UVEAL TRACT (UVEITIS & ENDOPHTHALMITIS)★★★★★

3.1 Applied Anatomy & Uveitis Classification

  • Uvea (Middle Layer): (1) Iris (controls light entry), (2) Ciliary Body (Pars plicata / Ciliary processes secrete Aqueous Humor into Posterior Chamber [located after iris]; Ciliary muscle = Accommodation; Pars plana = safe surgical entry), (3) Choroid. (🚫 Schwalbe's line & Bowman's membrane are NOT uvea!).
  • Anatomical Classification:
    • Anterior (Iritis/Iridocyclitis): Primary site = AC. ~50% are Idiopathic (#1 overall cause!).
    • Intermediate (Pars Planitis = idiopathic): Primary site = Vitreous $\rightarrow$ floaters, vitritis, "Snowball" opacities & snowbanking. Associated with Multiple Sclerosis (MS).
    • Posterior: Active Choroiditis = deep round yellow nodule; Active Retinitis = whitish retinal opacity (foveal lesion $\rightarrow$ central scotoma, least likely floaters; peripheral lesion $\rightarrow$ floaters).
    • Panuveitis: Entire uvea (VKH, Sympathetic ophthalmia, Behçet).
  • Course: Acute = sudden, lasts $\le$6 weeks | Chronic = lasts >3 months.
  • Pathology: Granulomatous = Large greasy Mutton-fat KPs + Koeppe's nodules (pupillary border) & Busacca's nodules (stroma) — seen in Sarcoidosis (bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy), TB, Syphilis, VKH, Sympathetic Ophthalmia.
  • Masquerade Syndromes: Malignant = Lymphoma, Melanoma | Non-neoplastic = Juvenile Xanthogranuloma, Drug-induced.

3.2 Acute Anterior Uveitis (Iridocyclitis) ★★★★★

  • Symptoms: Dull aching pain (worse at night), photophobia, watery tearing, and Diminution of vision (Constant symptom!). (🚫 NO mucopurulent discharge!).
  • 5 Basic Clinical Signs:
    1. Ciliary Injection (circumcorneal flush).
    2. Aqueous Flare & Cells (Earliest feature & surest sign of active inflammation! Seen by Slit-lamp Biomicroscope).
    3. Keratic Precipitates (KPs) on corneal endothelium (Most diagnostic sign!).
    4. Muddy Iris with loss of pattern.
    5. Small, Constricted (Miosis), Irregular Pupil (becomes Festooned with atropine due to Posterior Synechiae between iris & anterior lens).
  • IOP in Uveitis: Can be increased OR decreased.
  • Complications of Chronic Iridocyclitis:
    1. Complicated Cataract (PSC — #1 most common complication!).
    2. Posterior Synechiae $\rightarrow$ Seclusio Pupillae $\rightarrow$ Iris Bombé $\rightarrow$ Secondary Angle-Closure Glaucoma; Occlusio Pupillae (white membrane across pupil in JIA).
    3. Rubeosis Iridis (new iris vessels), Band keratopathy, CME, & Phthisis bulbi.
  • Treatment:
    • 1. Cycloplegics (Atropine 1% — Drug of Choice!): Relieves ciliary pain & prevents/breaks Posterior Synechiae. (🚫 Miotics [Pilocarpine] are STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED!).
    • 2. Corticosteroids (#1 anti-inflammatory): Treat inflammation & prevent CME (🚫 Do NOT prevent synechiae!). Start specific posterior uveitis Rx when CME present & VA falls to <6/12.
    • 3. Uveitic Glaucoma Rx: Steroids + Atropine + Beta-blockers / CAIs (🚫 Never Miotics!).

3.4 Acute Bacterial Endophthalmitis ★★★★

  • Definition & Incidence: Purulent inflammation of internal eye cavities/coats (aqueous/vitreous) sparing sclera (~1:1,000 post-cataract surgery; #1 source = patient's own external flora).
  • Clinical Features (24–72h post-op): Severe pain, visual loss, lid edema, hypopyon, vitritis, and Absent Red Reflex.
  • Rx: Vitreous aspirate (store overnight at 37°C!) + Immediate Intravitreal Antibiotics (Vancomycin + Ceftazidime; 🚫 Systemic antibiotics are NOT beneficial!) + Pars Plana Vitrectomy (PPV) ONLY if vision is Perception of Light (PL)!
4. THE SCLERA (SCLERITIS & OCULAR STAPHYLOMAS)★★★★

4.1 Blue Sclera, Scleritis & Scleral Rupture

  • Blue Sclera Causes: Osteogenesis Imperfecta, Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome, Marfan, Buphthalmos.
  • Episcleritis: Benign, superficial; blanches with phenylephrine. Differs from phlycten in being tender & flat.
  • Diffused / Nodular Scleritis: Severe deep granulomatous scleral inflammation $\rightarrow$ intense "boring" pain waking patient at night; violaceous (no blanching).
    • #1 Systemic Association: Rheumatoid Arthritis (causes Scleritis, Episcleritis, Corneal melts, Sjögren's; 🚫 NOT ischemic optic atrophy!).
  • Blunt Scleral Rupture: Weakest site = Superonasal limbus at Canal of Schlemm (blunt hit from down & out ruptures sclera Up & In!). Sure sign = Prolapsed intraocular contents.

5. THE VITREOUS HUMOR★★★★
  • Anatomy: Avascular gel (99% water, collagen, hyaluronic acid); Volume = 4 mL.
  • Muscae Volitantes: Floaters in myopia/senility. Acute PVD causes Photopsia (flashes) + floaters $\rightarrow$ risk of Retinal Detachment.
  • Vitreous Hemorrhage Causes:
    • #1 Overall Cause (Adults): Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy (PDR)!
    • #1 Cause in Young Adults: Ocular Trauma (and Eales' disease).
  • Signs & Rx: Sudden painless visual loss + Dim/Absent Red Reflex $\rightarrow$ do B-Scan Ultrasound (rule out RD) $\rightarrow$ Bed rest (head elevated 45°) $\rightarrow$ Pars Plana Vitrectomy (PPV) if non-clearing or RD.

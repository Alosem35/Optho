🧠 BIG-PICTURE MIND MAP (1-MINUTE OVERVIEW)⚡
1. ANATOMY & ANOMALIES • Lens: Avascular, grows throughout life! Power = 17D. A-scan biometry measures axial length for IOL.
• Coloboma: Equator notching + iris coloboma/giant tear.
• Lenticonus: Ant = Alport; Post = Lowe.
• Microspherophakia: Weill-Marchesani.
• Subluxation: Marfan, Homocystinuria, Weill-Marchesani (🚫 NOT Laurence-Moon-Biedl!).
2. CONGENITAL & LEUKOCORIA • Idiopathic: 33%.
• Lamellar (Zonular): AD, riders; Galactosemia, Hypocalcemia.
• Posterior Polar: At Nodal Point $\rightarrow$ severe vision drop!
• Leukocoria DD: Cataract, Retinoblastoma (13q14), ROP, PHPV, Toxocara (🚫 NOT Buphthalmos, Optic atrophy, or Choroidal melanoma!).
• Rx: Operate immediately (at 2 wks) to prevent Amblyopia!
3. SENILE & SECONDARY • Nuclear: Index myopia ("Second sight").
• Cortical: Spoke-like; Mature = entire cortex opaque (HM vision).
• PSC: Glare in bright sunlight/reading; caused by Steroids (#1), Uveitis, High Myopia, RP.
• Specific: Snowflake = Young Type 1 DM; Shield = Atopic dermatitis (10%); Rosette = Blunt trauma; Sunflower = Wilson's; Granules = Chlorpromazine.
4. GLAUCOMA & SURGERY • Phacomorphic: Intumescent $\rightarrow$ closed angle.
• Phacolytic: Hypermature $\rightarrow$ open angle. Rx: Cataract Extraction!
• Phacoemulsification (#1): 3 mm incision $\rightarrow$ Capsulorrhexis $\rightarrow$ Hydrodissection $\rightarrow$ Sculpt/Crack $\rightarrow$ Emulsify $\rightarrow$ PCIOL in Capsular Bag.
• Vitreous Loss: Anterior vitrectomy + ACIOL.
• PCO (Elschnig): Nd:YAG Laser Capsulotomy.
1. LENS PHYSIOLOGY, CONGENITAL ANOMALIES & ECTOPIA LENTIS★★★★

1.1 Applied Anatomy & Developmental Anomalies

  • Core Facts: Avascular, derived from surface ectoderm; continues to grow throughout lifetime! Affects refraction via: Sclerosis, Subluxation, or Dislocation.
  • A-Scan Ultrasound Biometry: Calculates IOL power by measuring Axial Length of the eye (+ Keratometry). (Note: Fluorescein angiography is NOT part of routine cataract workup!).
  • Lens Coloboma: Localized flattening/notching of lens equator due to zonular absence (associated with iris coloboma & giant retinal tear).
  • Anterior Lenticonus: Cone protrusion of anterior pole $\rightarrow$ Alport Syndrome.
  • Posterior Lenticonus & Microphakia: Bilateral $\rightarrow$ Lowe Syndrome.
  • Microspherophakia: Small spherical lens $\rightarrow$ pupillary block glaucoma $\rightarrow$ Weill-Marchesani Syndrome (🚫 NOT Alport!).

1.2 Ectopia Lentis (Subluxation & Dislocation)

  • Systemic Causes of Displaced Lens: Marfan Syndrome, Weill-Marchesani Syndrome, Homocystinuria, Trauma. (🚫 Displaced lens does NOT occur in Laurence-Moon-Biedl Syndrome!).
  • Signs of Lens Subluxation: Phacodonesis, Iridodonesis, Irregular AC depth, high astigmatism, and Uniocular Diplopia (lens equator visible in pupil; diplopia disappears on covering the injured eye!). (🚫 "Intact all zonules" is FALSE!).
  • Tremulous Iris (Iridodonesis) Seen In: Aphakia, Lens subluxation, Posterior lens dislocation, Hypermature shrunken cataract.
  • Glaucoma Inversus (Inverse Glaucoma): Pupillary block from anteriorly subluxated/dislocated lens or microspherophakia.
    • Rx: Improved with Mydriatics / Cycloplegics (Atropine) + Iridectomy / Lens extraction (🚫 Miotics [Pilocarpine] worsen it and are contraindicated!).
2. CONGENITAL CATARACT & LEUKOCORIA DIFFERENTIAL★★★★★

2.1 Congenital Cataract (Types & Associations)

  • Etiology: 33% (1/3) are Idiopathic; 1/3 Hereditary; 1/3 Maternal/Metabolic (Rubella, Galactosemia, Hypocalcemia, Lowe, Fabry).
  • Clinical Signs: Leukocoria (#1 cause of white reflex in children!), Nystagmus, Squint (Strabismus), poor fixation. (🚫 Proptosis is NOT a sign!).
  • Morphological Types (80-Q File Mastery):
    • Lamellar (Zonular): Autosomal Dominant; bilateral opacity with "Riders". Linked to Galactosemia & Hypocalcemia.
    • Posterior Polar: Lies right at the Nodal Point $\rightarrow$ markedly affects vision (worse in daylight!). Linked to persistent hyaloid remnants, PHPV, posterior lenticonus (🚫 NOT Fabry disease & does NOT cause glaucoma!).
    • Anterior Polar: Pyramidal opacity + persistent pupillary membrane.
    • Coronary (Supranuclear): Sporadic round deep cortical opacities.
    • Central Pulverulent: Non-progressive spheroidal nuclear opacities.
    • Sutural: Follows Y-suture; X-linked.
    • Blue-Dot: Common & innocuous | Spoke-like: Fabry disease.

2.2 Leukocoria DD & Treatment of Congenital Cataract

  • Differential Diagnosis of Leukocoria (Amaurotic Cat's Eye):
    1. Congenital Cataract (#1 most common overall in children).
    2. Retinoblastoma (#1 intraocular tumor in children; must rule out! 13q14 deletion; 75% calcified on X-ray/CT; Flexner-Wintersteiner rosettes; large unilateral $\rightarrow$ Enucleation).
    3. Retinopathy of Prematurity (ROP) & Persistent Hyperplastic Primary Vitreous (PHPV).
    4. Ocular Toxocariasis, Coats' Disease, Total Retinal Detachment, Endophthalmitis, & After-cataract.
    🚫 Leukocoria is NOT seen in: Congenital Glaucoma (Buphthalmos), Optic Atrophy, Acute Dacryocystitis, or Choroidal Malignant Melanoma!
  • Treatment of Congenital Cataract:
    • Timing: Dense cataract involving visual axis detected at birth / 2 weeks $\rightarrow$ Operate IMMEDIATELY (at 2 weeks) to prevent irreversible Amblyopia!
    • Lines of Rx: (1) Surgical: Lensectomy / Aspiration + Posterior Capsulotomy + Anterior Vitrectomy, (2) Optical: IOL (if age-appropriate), Contact lenses, or Glasses, (3) Amblyopia Rx: Occlusion (patching) of good eye.
3. SENILE, METABOLIC, TRAUMATIC & COMPLICATED CATARACTS★★★★★
Cataract Type Key Pathophysiology & Morphology Visual Complaint & Exam Buzzwords
1. Nuclear Senile Central nuclear sclerosis/yellowing $\rightarrow$ increases refractive index $\rightarrow$ Index Myopia. "Second Sight" (presbyopic patient reads better without glasses!) + gradual change in glasses.
2. Cortical Senile Vacuoles & water clefts $\rightarrow$ radial wedge-shaped spoke-like opacities. Glare, fixed black spots, uniocular diplopia.
3. Posterior Subcapsular (PSC) Axial opacity just anterior to posterior capsule (near Nodal Point).
Causes: Topical/Systemic Steroids (#1!), Chronic Anterior Uveitis, High Myopia, Retinitis Pigmentosa.		 Worse vision in bright daylight/sunlight, glare, & difficulty reading (due to pupillary miosis!).
4. Christmas-Tree Polychromatic needle-like deep cortical crystals (may coexist with other opacities; Myotonic dystrophy). Glittering multi-colored needles on slit-lamp.
5. Diabetic Cataract • Young Type 1 DM: Rapid bilateral Snowflake punctate opacities.
• Adult DM: Cortical & subcapsular opacities.		 Acute hyperglycemia also causes reversible Index Myopia.
6. Specific Syndromic / Drug • Atopic Dermatitis (10%): Bilateral anterior subcapsular Shield Cataract.
• Wilson's Disease (Chalcosis): Copper Sunflower Cataract.
• Drugs: Chlorpromazine = central anterior capsular granules | Acute Glaucoma = Glaukomflecken.		 Blunt Concussion Trauma: Rosette-shaped (flower-shaped) posterior cataract + Vossius Ring on anterior capsule! Physical agent = Ionizing radiation (post-maxillary tumor radiotherapy).
7. Complicated (Secondary) Local intraocular disease: Chronic Iridocyclitis (#1), High Myopia, Retinitis Pigmentosa, Retinal Detachment, Retinoblastoma. (🚫 NOT caused by Blepharitis, Disciform keratitis, or Electric shock!). Posterior subcapsular rosette opacity with Polychromatic luster.

3.2 Stages of Senile Cortical Cataract & Lens-Induced Glaucoma ★★★★★

  • 1. Immature (ISC): Partial cortical opacities; iris shadow present.
  • 2. Intumescent (Swollen): Hydrated swollen lens $\rightarrow$ Shallow Anterior Chamber $\rightarrow$ causes Phacomorphic Glaucoma (secondary acute closed-angle glaucoma via pupillary block).
  • 3. Mature (MSC): Cortical cataract involving the ENTIRE cortex (completely white opaque lens).
    • Criteria: Vision reduced to Hand Movements (HM), Absent Red Reflex, Absent Iris Shadow.
    • Testing HM Eye: Macular function = Color Perception | Visual field = Light Projection (🚫 Light perception is NOT a field test!).
  • 4. Hypermature (HMC):
    • Morgagnian Cataract: Liquefied cortex + lens nucleus sinks inferiorly.
    • Phacolytic Glaucoma: Leaking lens proteins engulfed by macrophages clog open trabecular meshwork.
  • Summary of Lens-Induced Glaucomas: Include Phacomorphic, Phacolytic, Phacoanaphylactic, and Lens dislocation. (🚫 Least likely / does NOT occur in: Posterior Subcapsular, Posterior Polar, Nuclear Sclerosis, or Neovascular glaucoma!).
  • Definitive Rx of Phacomorphic & Phacolytic Glaucoma: Control IOP medically $\rightarrow$ Urgent Cataract Extraction!
4. CATARACT SURGERY: TECHNIQUES & COMPLICATIONS★★★★★

4.1 Indications & Surgical Techniques

  • Primary Indication: Early cataract that compromises patient's daily activities (🚫 Do NOT wait until cataract matures!).
  • Absolute Contraindication: Active Chronic Dacryocystitis (risk of pneumococcal endophthalmitis!).
  • Prophylaxis Before/After Surgery: Treat peripheral Lattice Degeneration to prevent Retinal Detachment!
  • 1. ICCE: Removes entire lens + capsule (NO lens capsule left behind!).
  • 2. ECCE: Nucleus expressed intact as a single piece through large incision $\rightarrow$ cortical cleanup $\rightarrow$ polishing posterior capsule $\rightarrow$ IOL haptics placed inside the Capsular Bag (avoids IOL decentration). (🚫 "Sculpting of nucleus" is Phaco, NOT ECCE!).
  • 3. Phacoemulsification (Preferred #1 — Earliest Visual Rehabilitation!):
    • Incision Size: 3 mm sutureless incision (faster healing, less astigmatism; 🚫 NOT better than ECCE for extremely hard cataracts!).
    • Exact Sequence of Steps: (1) Capsulorrhexis (continuous curvilinear opening in ant. capsule) $\rightarrow$ (2) Hydrodissection (separating lens from capsule with fluid) $\rightarrow$ (3) Sculpting & Cracking $\rightarrow$ (4) Emulsification of each quadrant $\rightarrow$ (5) Cortical aspiration $\rightarrow$ (6) PCIOL in Capsular Bag.

4.2 Intraoperative & Postoperative Complications ★★★★★

  • 1. Intraoperative Complications:
    • Accidental Aspiration of Posterior Capsule: Ruptures hyaloid face $\rightarrow$ Vitreous Loss $\rightarrow$ Rx: Anterior vitrectomy + constrict pupil + peripheral iridectomy + glide insertion of Anterior Chamber IOL (ACIOL) (🚫 Do NOT do pars plana vitrectomy before ACIOL!).
    • Dropped Nuclear Fragment (>20–30%) posteriorly: Refer for Pars Plana Vitrectomy (PPV).
    • Suprachoroidal (Expulsive) Hemorrhage: Immediate wound closure + IV Hyperosmotic agent (Mannitol); 7–14d later drain blood + PPV + air-fluid exchange (🚫 Do NOT repeat hyperosmotic agent at 7–14d!).
  • 2. Early Post-op:
    • Iris Prolapse: Iris visible in wound (caused by inappropriate management of vitreous loss).
    • Striate Keratopathy: Corneal edema + Descemet folds (persistent cases / Fuchs' endothelial dystrophy $\rightarrow$ Penetrating Keratoplasty [PKP]).
    • Acute Endophthalmitis (1:1,000; 24–72h): From patient's external flora. Pain, hypopyon, absent red reflex $\rightarrow$ Intravitreal Antibiotics + PPV if vision is PL.
  • 3. Late Post-op — Posterior Capsule Opacification (PCO / After-Cataract):
    • #1 Most common cause of decreased vision after ECCE / Phaco!
    • Types: Fibrosis type (2–6 months) vs. Elschnig's Pearls (years later).
    • Rx of Choice: Nd:YAG Laser Capsulotomy. (Complications: Uncommon CME, and Retinal Detachment in High Myopia).

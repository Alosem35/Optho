2. CONJUNCTIVA & DRY EYE (XEROSIS)★★★★★

2.1 Infectious Conjunctivitis

  • Normal Commensal Flora: Corynebacterium xerosis, Staph. epidermidis.
  • Mucopurulent: Staph. aureus (#1 overall bacterial) or Koch-Weeks bacillus (H. aegyptius) (#1 epidemic). Yellow crusts gluing lashes in morning. (Persistent unilateral conjunctivitis $\rightarrow$ rule out Chronic Dacryocystitis!).
  • Purulent (Hyperacute): Neisseria gonorrhoeae $\rightarrow$ copious pus $\rightarrow$ invades intact corneal epithelium.
  • True Membranous: Corynebacterium diphtheriae $\rightarrow$ fibrinous membrane peels with bleeding $\rightarrow$ heals with Symblepharon & paralytic squint.
  • Angular Blepharoconjunctivitis:
    • Organism: Morax-Axenfeld diplobacillus (Moraxella lacunata) $\rightarrow$ proteolytic enzyme macerates skin at inner & outer canthi.
    • Rx of Choice: Zinc sulfate/oxide drops (inhibits enzyme) + Oxytetracycline ointment.
  • Adenoviral (Epidemic) Keratoconjunctivitis:
    • #1 Viral cause: Adenovirus.
    • Classic Vignette: Child/adult with fever & sore throat + excessive watery discharge + lower tarsal follicles + tender preauricular lymphadenopathy (+ subconjunctival hemorrhage).
  • Ophthalmia Neonatorum (First 28 days of life):
    • Causes: N. gonorrhoeae (Days 2–4; most dangerous!), Chlamydia trachomatis (TRIC — Days 5–14; most common), Staph. aureus, HSV-2. (🚫 NOT caused by birth trauma or Adenovirus!).
    • Prophylaxis: Crede's 1% Silver Nitrate drops or Povidone-iodine / Penicillin drops.
  • 🚫 NEVER Bandage (Patch) the Eye in Mucopurulent / Purulent Conjunctivitis! (Traps pus/heat $\rightarrow$ corneal ulceration).

2.2 Trachoma (Chlamydia trachomatis A–C) ★★★★

🔬 Obligate intracellular bacteria $\rightarrow$ H.P. intracytoplasmic inclusion bodies $\rightarrow$ chronic upper tarsal inflammation $\rightarrow$ subepithelial fibrosis & cicatrization.
  • Active Infectious Signs (Contagious ONLY at this stage!):
    • Upper tarsal expressible follicles & papillae.
    • Superior limbal follicles (Herbert's Rosettes).
    • Active Pannus: Vessels + infiltrate in upper 1/3 of cornea lying between Bowman's membrane & Epithelium.
  • Cicatricial (Healed) Pathognomonic Signs:
    • Arlt's Line: Horizontal fibrous scar in upper palpebral conjunctiva.
    • Herbert's Pits: Depressed pits at superior limbus (healed rosettes).
    • Post-trachomatous concretions (calcified debris in Henle's glands).
  • WHO Classification of Trachoma (FISTO):
    1. TF: Follicular ($\ge$5 follicles >0.5 mm on upper tarsus).
    2. TI: Intense inflammatory thickening obscuring >50% of tarsal vessels.
    3. TS: Trachomatous Scarring (Arlt's line).
    4. TT: Trachomatous Trichiasis ($\ge$1 lash rubbing cornea).
    5. CO: Corneal Opacity (#1 cause of vision loss/blindness in Trachoma!).
    🚫 Trap: "Pannus" and "Trantas spots" are NOT in the WHO FISTO classification!
  • Complications (Corneal Damage Mechanisms): Upper lid Trichiasis (#1 cause!), Cicatricial Entropion, Xerosis (goblet/lacrimal duct destruction), Symblepharon, Lagophthalmos/exposure, and Upper Corneal Ulcer.
  • Rx: Medical is effective! Oral Azithromycin (single dose) or Topical Tetracycline 1% ointment + Surgery for TT.

2.4 Degenerations (Pterygium) & Symblepharon ★★★★

  • Pinguecula: Yellowish perilimbal nodule; Elastoid hyaline degeneration (🚫 NOT fatty!).
  • Pterygium: Triangular degenerative fibrovascular encroachment of bulbar conjunctiva onto cornea (nasal > temporal).
    • Etiology: Chronic Ultraviolet (UV) radiation, hot/dry/dusty climate $\rightarrow$ elastotic degeneration destroying Bowman's membrane. (🚫 NOT a tumor, NOT viral, NOT immunocompromised!).
    • Effects: Cosmetic, With-the-rule / Irregular Astigmatism, Visual loss (if crosses pupil), Diplopia (restricts medial rectus).
    • Rx: Surgical excision + Conjunctival Autograft. Prevent recurrence with: Mitomycin-C / 5-FU, Beta-irradiation, Topical Steroids (🚫 Topical antibiotics do NOT prevent recurrence!).
  • True Pterygium vs. Pseudopterygium:
    • Pterygium: Degenerative, horizontal (3/9 o'clock), progressive, Probe CANNOT pass under neck.
    • Pseudopterygium: Inflammatory fold adherent to marginal ulcer/burn, any meridian, stationary, Probe CAN pass underneath neck!
  • Symblepharon: Adhesion of palpebral to bulbar conjunctiva (Alkali burns, Diphtheria, Trachoma, Post-pterygium excision). Complications: Lagophthalmos, Binocular Diplopia (1 month post-pterygium surgery!), Dry eye, Entropion.

2.5 Dry Eye (Xerosis), Bitot's Spot & Keratomalacia ★★★★

  • Etiology of Xerosis (4):
    1. Lacrimal Hyposecretion: Congenital alacrima, Senile atrophy, Sjögren's Syndrome (Autoimmune: Dry eye/Xerophthalmia + Dry mouth + Rheumatoid arthritis).
    2. Cicatricial / Mucin Deficiency: Trachoma, Chemical burns, Diphtheria, Pemphigoid.
    3. Exposure: Lagophthalmos (CN VII palsy), Severe Proptosis, Ectropion.
    4. Nutritional: Vitamin A Deficiency.
  • Vitamin A Deficiency Spectrum (WHO):
    • XN: Night Blindness (Nyctalopia) — #1 earliest symptom (rod rhodopsin deficiency).
    • X1B (Bitot's Spot): Silvery-white foamy triangular keratinized plaque on temporal bulbar conjunctiva (+ C. xerosis gas).
    • X3B (Keratomalacia): Acute bilateral painless full-thickness liquefactive corneal melting in marasmic children (triggered by Measles) $\rightarrow$ perforation, Anterior Staphyloma, Phthisis bulbi. (Corneal involvement is the cause of blindness in Vit A def!).
  • Diagnosis of Dry Eye: Schirmer's Test (<5 mm in 5 min = severe dry eye), TBUT (<10 sec), Rose Bengal (stains dead cells).
  • Rx: Mild = Topical Lubricants (Artificial tears) + Punctal plugs | Vit A def = Urgent Systemic Vitamin A (200,000 IU Days 1, 2, 14) + Protein diet + Atropine/Antibiotics.
3. THE CORNEA (KERATITIS, KERATOCONUS & SURGERY)★★★★★

3.1 Applied Corneal Anatomy & Physiology

  • Refractive Power: ~43 D = 2/3 of total eye power. Anterior radius = 8.0 mm (increasing radius to 8.2 mm flattens cornea $\rightarrow$ causes Hypermetropia).
  • 5 Layers (Ant $\rightarrow$ Post):
    1. Epithelium: Non-keratinized stratified squamous (regenerates without scar).
    2. Bowman's Membrane: Acellular collagen; never regenerates (scars).
    3. Substantia Propria (Stroma): Forms 90% of thickness; regular collagen lattice (heals with irregular fibers $\rightarrow$ opacity).
    4. Descemet's Membrane: Tough elastic membrane; resistant to destruction & CAN regenerate (herniates as Descemetocele).
    5. Endothelium: Single layer with active Na+/K+ pump; examined by Specular Microscope. Injury $\rightarrow$ immediate Corneal Edema (increased thickness, cloudy, bullae, vascularization; 🚫 does NOT increase corneal diameter in adults!).
  • Transparency Factors: Regular stromal collagen lattice, Avascular/no lymphatics, Unmyelinated nerves, Endothelial pump, Normal IOP.
  • 3 Nutrition Sources: Air (O2), Aqueous humor (Glucose), Perilimbal capillaries. (🚫 NOT Vitreous & NOT Anterior ciliary arteries!).
  • Nerves & Reflex: Sensory = CN V1. Corneal Touch Reflex: CN V (afferent) + CN VII (efferent).
  • Metrics: Thickness = Pachymetry | Power/Shape = Keratometry & Topography | Diameter = Caliper & Ruler.

3.2 Bacterial Corneal Ulcers & Hypopyon Ulcer ★★★★★

🔬 4 Bacteria Invading INTACT Epithelium: N. gonorrhoeae, C. diphtheriae, N. meningitidis, Haemophilus/Listeria. (🚫 Fungi CANNOT invade intact epithelium!).
  • Symptoms: Pain (earliest!), Photophobia, Blepharospasm, Watery tearing, Decreased vision (🚫 NOT mucopurulent discharge!).
  • Sure Diagnostic Sign: +ve Fluorescein Stain (turns GREEN).
  • Hypopyon Facts: Pus (leukocytes + fibrin) in AC caused by bacterial toxin diffusion $\rightarrow$ STERILE (contains NO bacteria!) & absorbs with therapy.
  • Typical Hypopyon Ulcer (Ulcus Serpens): Caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae (Pneumococcus — #1!) (source = Chronic Dacryocystitis!). Central disc ulcer + coagulated pyramidal hypopyon + early perforation (Descemetocele is uncommon with Pneumococcus!).
  • Contact Lens Ulcer (#1): Pseudomonas aeruginosa (fulminant melting).
  • Moment of Perforation: Sudden cessation of pain + aqueous gush + flat AC.
  • Complications:
    • Non-perforating: Toxic iridocyclitis, Secondary glaucoma, Descemetocele, Scars (Nebula affects vision/glare more if central vs Dense Leucoma blocks light).
    • Perforating: Leucoma Adherent (iris incarcerated in dense scar), Peripheral Anterior Synechiae (from small peripheral perforation), Corneal Fistula (+ve Seidel test), Anterior polar cataract, Anterior staphyloma, Endophthalmitis. (🚫 Vitreous hemorrhage is NOT a typical complication!).
  • Rx: Scrape for Gram/Culture + Intensive hourly Fortified Antibiotics + Atropine 1% Cycloplegic (Drug ALWAYS indicated in any ulcer!) + Vit A/C. (🚫 NEVER give Topical Steroids $\rightarrow$ Perforation!).

3.4 Keratoconus (Conical Cornea) ★★★★★

  • Definition: Bilateral non-inflammatory central/paracentral corneal thinning & conical ectasia starting at puberty $\rightarrow$ causes High Myopia & Irregular Astigmatism (🚫 Never Hypermetropia!).
  • 7 Classic Signs:
    1. Ophthalmoscopy: "Oil-droplet" red reflex.
    2. Retinoscopy: Scissoring red reflex.
    3. Slit-lamp: Central thinning, Vogt's striae (vertical lines), & Fleischer's ring (epithelial Iron deposit at cone base).
    4. Downgaze: Munson's sign (V-shaped lower lid bulge).
    5. Acute Hydrops: Sudden corneal edema from rupture of Descemet's membrane (🚫 Hutchinson's sign is HZO, NOT keratoconus!).
  • Gold Standard Investigation: Corneal Topography (Pentacam) (+ Pachymetry for thinning, Keratometry >48 D).
  • Stepwise Management:
    1. Halt Progression: Corneal Collagen Cross-Linking (CXL) (Riboflavin + UV-A).
    2. Optical Correction: Early = Glasses $\rightarrow$ Moderate = Rigid Gas Permeable (Hard) Contact Lenses (🚫 Advanced keratoconus is LEAST corrected by Spectacles!).
    3. Surgical: Intracorneal Ring Segments (ICRS) $\rightarrow$ Advanced scarring = Penetrating Keratoplasty (PKP) / DALK.
    4. 🚫 LASIK is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in Keratoconus!
4. OPTICS & ERRORS OF REFRACTION★★★★★
🔬 Core Optics: Emmetropia = parallel rays focus on retina at rest. | Ametropia = Myopia, Hyperopia, Astigmatism (#1 cause of defective vision in school children!). | Primary Cause of Refractive Errors: Change in Axial Length. | Nodal Point: Optical center just anterior to posterior lens capsule (Rays passing through Nodal Point do NOT undergo refraction!). | Angle Alpha: Angle between Optic & Visual axes at nodal point (+5° normal; >5° in Hyperopia; negative in High Myopia). | Accommodation (CN III): Max in Childhood; ciliary muscle contracts $\rightarrow$ lens becomes thicker/more convex (🚫 NEVER thinner!), AC shallows, pupil constricts.

4.2 Astigmatism, Presbyopia, Anisometropia & Aphakia

  • Astigmatism: Parallel rays form two focal lines (no single point focus) due to unequal curvature across meridians.
    • Regular (Meridians 90° apart): With-the-rule = Vertical meridian more curved (commonest).
      • Simple Regular (1 emmetropic, 1 ametropic) $\rightarrow$ Rx: Cylindrical Lenses.
      • Compound Regular (both myopic or both hyperopic, e.g., +2.00 +2.00 x 180) $\rightarrow$ Rx: Sphero-Cylindrical Lenses.
    • Irregular: Causes = Keratoconus, Corneal scars/opacities, Pterygium. Rx: Rigid Contact Lenses or Keratoplasty (🚫 Cannot be corrected with glasses!).
  • Presbyopia: Physiological age-related loss of accommodation (>40y) due to lens sclerosis. Affected by: Age, Refractive error, Working distance (🚫 Not found in children or aphakia; not sight-threatening!).
    • Rx: Convex / Bifocal glasses. (Pearl: Frequent change of presbyopic glasses = early sign of Open-Angle Glaucoma!).
  • Anisometropia: Significant refractive difference between both eyes $\rightarrow$ causes Aniseikonia & Unilateral Amblyopia. Rx: Contact Lenses (🚫 Not best corrected with glasses if >3 D!).
  • Aphakia: Absence of lens $\rightarrow$ high hyperopia, deep AC, Tremulous iris (Iridodonesis).
    • Rx of Choice: Posterior Chamber IOL (PCIOL) in the Capsular Bag (or Contact lens / ACIOL).
    • 🚫 TOP EXAM TRAP: Unilateral Aphakia CANNOT be corrected by Spectacles (Glasses)! (+10 D glasses cause 25–30% image magnification $\rightarrow$ intractable Binocular Diplopia!).
  • LASIK Contraindications: Age <18y (e.g., 15 years old!), unstable refraction, thin cornea, or Keratoconus.
